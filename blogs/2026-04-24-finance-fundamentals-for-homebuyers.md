<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Let’s Start With the Basics </h2>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">How Much Should I Save for a Down Payment? </h3>



<p>Conventional loans require a minimum 3% of the home purchase price. When placing <a href="https://www.investopedia.com/terms/d/down_payment.asp" rel="noopener" target="_blank">20% down</a> on a Conventional loan, you avoid the need for Private Mortgage Insurance. FHA loans require as little as 3.5% down. Some VA and USDA loans offer no down payment options for eligible buyers.&nbsp;</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">What is an Interest Rate and How Does It Affect My Monthly Payment? </h3>



<p>An interest rate is the cost you pay to borrow money from a lender. It’s shown as a percentage and is added to your monthly mortgage payment.&nbsp;</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Okay, So How Much Home Can I Afford? </h3>



<p>This depends on your income, monthly expenses, and how much you have saved for a down payment. Taylor Morrison Home Funding, Inc., can give you a personalized picture of what will work for you. </p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">What is the Difference Between Pre-Application and Loan Approval? </h3>



<p>A pre-application is a quick estimate about your income, debts, and credit—not a commitment to lend or guaranteed offer of any specific loan program. It gives you an idea of how much you might be able to borrow. <div>A loan approval is a more thorough process where the lender verifies your financial documents and credit. You are required to cooperate in providing certain required support within the timeframe necessary. </div></p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">What are Closing Costs and How Much are They? </h3>



<p>Closing costs are the fees and expenses you pay when finalizing your home purchase. They cover items like origination charges, appraisal, title insurance and taxes. Typically, closing costs range from 2% &#8211; 5% of the home’s purchase price.&nbsp;</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">How Does Renting Vs. Buying Compare Financially in the Long Term? </h3>



<h4 class="wp-block-heading">Renting&nbsp;</h4>



<p>Paying monthly rent without building ownership | Rent can increase over time, and you do not build equity or receive any applicable tax benefits. </p>



<h4 class="wp-block-heading">Buying a Home&nbsp;</h4>



<p>The chance to build equity in a property you own | Owning a home can turn your monthly payments into an asset for you.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Mortgage Types&nbsp;</h3>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Conventional: </strong>Standard financing option without government backing.&nbsp;</li>
</ul>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>FHA: </strong>Government-insured financing with a lower down payment and flexible qualifying guidelines.&nbsp;</li>
</ul>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>VA: </strong>Available to eligible veterans and active-duty service members, often with no down payment.&nbsp;</li>
</ul>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>USDA Financing: </strong>Offered at select communities, finance up to 100% of your home, helping you keep more cash in your pocket.&nbsp;</li>
</ul>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Beyond the Basics&nbsp;</h2>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">What is a Rate Buydown? </h3>



<p><a href="https://blog.taylormorrison.com/finance/rate-locks-and-buydowns-explained-in-simple-terms-tms-dollars-and-sense-series/">A rate buydown </a>is when you pay extra money upfront to get a lower interest rate, compared to market average, either for the first few years or the life of your loan. Taylor Morrison Home Funding, Inc., will cover the cost to buy down your rate. </p>
